PART I: COMPLAINANT STATEMENT (Shri M. Sundaram, Age 48)

"I am the Managing Director of Sri Balaji Garments Pvt Ltd, Anna Nagar. On 12-June-2023, 
I entered into a commercial agreement with the Accused, Rajesh Kumar (Proprietor of 
Precision Machines India), for the supply of two automated industrial textile printing 
machines. The total contract value was Rs. 25,00,000/-.

As agreed, on 15-June-2023, I transferred an advance payment of Rs. 15,00,000/- (Rupees

10-August-2023, the Accused issued a cheque for Rs. 5,00,000/- bearing No. 892110 
drawn on ICICI Bank, which was returned dishonoured due to 'Insufficient Funds'. 
The Accused has dishonestly misappropriated my funds. 

Meeting Details: The crucial dispute meeting was held on 10-August-2023 at 10:30 AM 
in my corporate office at Anna Nagar."

PART IV: APPLICABLE LEGAL PROVISIONS & CASE PRECEDENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Indian Penal Code Section 420: Requires dishonest intention at the inception 
   of the contract (Mens Rea). Mere failure to fulfill a promise or delayed supply 
   does not constitute cheating without fraudulent inducement from day one.
   - Precedent: Satishchandra Ratanlal Shah v. State of Gujarat (2019) 9 SCC 148 
     (Supreme Court held breach of commercial contract does not equal criminal cheating).

2. Indian Penal Code Section 406: Entrustment and dishonest conversion to own use. 
   Where money is paid as a contractual advance, it is a debtor-creditor relationship.

3. Code of Criminal Procedure Section 437 / Section 480 BNSS: Principles for granting bail:
   - Offence punishable with imprisonment under 7 years.
   - No flight risk or tampering with evidence.
   - Mandatory compliance of Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar (2014) 8 SCC 273 guidelines.
